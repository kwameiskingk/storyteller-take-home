Recommendation

What I am asking Lewis and Priya to agree at 10:00 on Friday.

Launch for Game 3 at reduced scope. Confirm or withdraw to the client on Monday 7 September against the acceptance criteria below. Pause the feed on the night if any stop condition fires.

Held lane, released when the live lane is clear

  • Previews and analysis

Official league and team feeds plus the approved broadcaster only. No CourtsideWire for this game. Every card is approved by a person before the client sees it. The client's own monitoring of official feeds stays on as the system of record for the event.

Why not full scope

In a ten-item replay the pilot produced a duplicate card from a retry bug, fabricated a record claim at 0.94 confidence, lost the player availability update without telling anyone, rejected the tipoff change as routine, and ranked a sponsor competition as high-priority player news. Forecast volume is 240 cards in six hours with peaks of 80 an hour. The operator reviews 30 to 40 an hour and leaves thirty minutes before tipoff.

Why not withdraw

The causes are specific and each has an owner. The duplicate and the lost item are bounded engineering fixes. The record claim, the rejected schedule change and the promotion are instruction and process changes already drafted and tested outside production. The client keeps a safety net. Withdrawing from a fixable problem gives up the one event Product wants to prove the service on.

CourtsideWire

The client is right that it was 22 minutes ahead on the Cole story. It also rested on two anonymous sources and said "expected to miss" where the team said "questionable". One item cannot show whether a source is reliable. Adding it needs an ingestion change, a change to what was promised to the client, and a product decision on how unconfirmed reports look. Not in eight days. After Game 3 I would run it in the background for four weeks and measure two things: how far ahead it is, and how often official sources later contradict it. Those numbers decide it.

Four of the nine approved sample items are live-lane card types. Applied to the forecast peak, with duplicates removed at source and promotions filtered, about 36 cards an hour reach the approver. That is the limit of one person at Sam's observed rate, with no slack for a hard case. Three consequences follow.

  1. A second approver from 18:30 UTC is an explicit ask of Lewis and Priya, not an assumption. Sam's agreed cover ends at 18:30; tipoff is 19:00.
  2. Queue dwell is a stop condition, not a hope.
  3. Monday's logged session replaces the 44 percent sample figure with a measured one before the client is told anything.

The ten-minute target

The client wants developments surfaced within about ten minutes of appearing in an approved source. The feed polls every five minutes. Generation took 7 to 21 seconds in the replay, about 36 with one timeout and retry. A straightforward review is about a minute. That leaves roughly three and a half minutes for a card to wait in the queue. Achievable for one approver who does nothing else. Not achievable if that person is also reconciling exports and handling escalations through the result peak.

Acceptance criteria and stop conditions

Lewis said no acceptance criteria exist. These are proposed as the first set.

Tuesday's 20-card labelled set is a second checkpoint. A fabricated fact or more than one duplicate withdraws the launch, and the client hears on Tuesday evening, before Wednesday's training.

On the night, each with how it is observed

  • A factual error reaches the client. Observed by client report or by the approver.
  • Cards wait more than ten minutes for 30 consecutive minutes. Observed from decision log timestamps against card generation times.
  • No approver online.
  • Two duplicate cards from one source in the queue, or retries per call above 0.25 over 30 minutes if Marta's 15-minute export ships. This is Finance's trigger, because spend cannot be seen live.

Any one of these pauses the feed. Priya sends a pre-written message and the editors continue with manual monitoring.

What would change the recommendation

  • To full scope: a clean 20-card set, Monday's session showing the slow-review share well under the assumed 20 percent, and Lewis releasing the held lane.
  • To withdraw: Marta ships item 1 only. The client will not keep manual monitoring. Monday's session shows Sam under 30 cards an hour with the checklist. Or no second approver and a measured live-lane share well above 44 percent.

Demonstration

The step I took as far as I could: make the evidence reproducible and the instructions testable outside production.

# 1. Join sources, log and queue; write the fact table and findings
python3 -I checks/reconcile.py data outputs
# 2. Compute every number quoted in the documents
python3 -I checks/numbers.py data outputs
# 3. Check both against hand-counted expectations
python3 -I checks/test_reconcile.py
# 4. Run current and revised instructions over all ten items, live, through the Claude Code CLI
python3 -I prompts/run_prompts.py data/01_source_content.json outputs/prompt_runs --model sonnet
# 5. Build the before-and-after comparison with an automatic claim check
python3 -I prompts/compare_runs.py data/01_source_content.json data/02_trial_recommendations.json outputs/prompt_runs outputs/prompt_before_after.md

Step 4 needs a logged-in Claude Code CLI. Its outputs are committed in outputs/prompt_runs/ and the comparison in prompt_before_after.md, so the result can be read without running it.

What it shows

  • The reconciliation derives the duplicate, the lost item, the rejected item, the latency pattern and the cost pattern from the raw files, quoting the rows.
  • The automatic claim check flags the two faulty pilot cards. It also flags "before Game 3" against "before Friday" on the promotion card, which a person overrules because Game 3 is on Friday. That is what the check is for: narrowing what a person must read, not replacing them.
  • The revised instructions, run today, produce a correct Mensah card, reject the sponsor competition as promotional, classify the tipoff change as a high-priority schedule change, keep "questionable" and "sources say", and return a list of claims for the approver instead of a confidence score. For Mensah the list reads "the record is tied, not broken" and "franchise record, not a league record".

What it does not show

The current instructions, run on today's model, also got most things right. So the pilot's errors are partly model and pipeline behaviour, and this test proves the instructions are better specified, not that production will behave the same. Only Marta's rerun through the deployed path proves that. It is acceptance criterion 2 for that reason.

How I used AI and how I checked it

Full chronological log, with the representative prompt and output, in ai_log/ai_use_log.md.

Claude Code did the heavy lifting: reading the pack, writing the scripts, tests and instructions, and first drafts of every document. I directed, questioned, re-read, and decided. The method was build, then cold review by a separate AI session that had not seen our conclusions, then fix, then repeat on the finished version.

Checked

  • Every finding cites a row in the files. Tests encode hand counts written before the script ran. Every figure in prose is computed by a script.
  • Two independent AI reviews: a cold read of the pack, then a simulated hiring panel grading the first full draft. The panel found a remit violation in the client update, an impossible gate, unobservable stop conditions, two factual errors and eight contradictions. All are fixed in this version.
  • A scan of the pack for hidden characters, direction overrides, embedded markup and unexpected fields. Clean.

Changed

  • The client update, rewritten to precede Lewis's decision rather than pre-empt it.
  • The Monday gate, rebuilt around Friday's rerun and Monday's session after the first version depended on Tuesday's data.
  • Stop conditions, rebuilt around what can be observed on the night.
  • The revised instruction's time-zone rule, moved out of headlines after it produced "(zone not stated)" in a title.

Rejected

  • The AI's first framing of the duplicate as a prompt problem. The log shows a retry bug.
  • A hypothesis from the first review that the client's complaint referred to earlier output. The pack says the client was sent a selection from this trial. I had adopted it; the second review caught it.
  • The AI's capacity model of 50 cards an hour, in favour of Sam's observed 30 to 40.

Limitations

The prompt test proves instructions, not production. Outputs vary between runs. The story key is not a deduplication key. The AI wrote 8x where the figure is 5x and 45 percent where it is 44, and listed a pack fact as an assumption; the numbers script and the second review caught all three.
